# Shub-Niggurath, the Young, and Porneia: research dossier for Final Protocol, battle group 1

Research date: 2026-11-14. Method: 32 web searches and about 60 page fetches. The search budget for this run ran out before every point could be double-checked; anything not confirmed from a page I actually read is marked "unverified" or "search summary only". Quotations from fetched pages are short because the fetch tool truncates long quotes; check wording against the linked primary text before shipping any line in the game.

## Summary

- **Lovecraft never shows Shub-Niggurath.** In every Lovecraft text I read she is only a name shouted in a ritual or by a raving man ("Iä! Shub-Niggurath! The Goat with a Thousand Young!"). The director's rule "the mother never appears" is therefore the most faithful treatment possible, not a deviation [1][2][3][8][9].
- **Her only description comes from one letter**: Lovecraft to Willis Conover, 1 September 1936, calls her "the hellish cloud-like" wife of Yog-Sothoth [13][14]. A cloud whose young are countless is a ready-made image for cloud computing and model instances. Use the idea; do not quote the letter at length (it was first published in 1976).
- **The tree-with-hooves monster is not Lovecraft's.** It comes from Robert Bloch's "Notebook Found in a Deserted House" (Weird Tales, May 1951), where the boy narrator calls it a "shoggoth" [19][20]. Chaosium's Call of Cthulhu RPG (1981, Sandy Petersen) renamed that creature the "Dark Young of Shub-Niggurath" [22][23]. Bloch's story is under US copyright until 2047 (renewal RE0000036952) [21] and Chaosium claims its creature names and descriptions as its IP [24][25]. **Do not use the name "Dark Young" or copy the ropy-tentacle / puckered-mouth / green-goo design.** Build our own creature from the public-domain phrase "a Thousand Young".
- **Do not call the boss "the Kid" in a chapter about lust.** A child-word next to porneia invites a reading nobody wants, and real 2025-2026 scandals are precisely about sexualised child-coded AI output [49][57]. Use a caprine word: THE YOUNG, YEANLING, or a count such as "1 OF 1000".
- **The mother's name is itself a live problem.** In October 2026 Chaosium renamed her "Shub-Gorath" in its board game *Old Ones Origins* because the name resembles a racial slur and trips automated content filters (the "Scunthorpe problem"), while saying scholars agree the name was not derived from the slur [26][12]. A web game faces the same filters. Recommended: the mother's name never renders in full in our game; the sandbox's own filter masks it. That turns a liability into the chapter's central meta device.
- **Porneia originally named the one who was sold.** The word comes from the prostitution word group (root sense "to sell") and is rare in classical Greek; Kyle Harper argues it first described the seller's activity and only later, in Jewish and Christian use, became the buyer's sin and then a catch-all for sexual wrongdoing [31][34]. For an AI rented by the hour, this is the theme in one word.
- **The monastic definition already reads like a chatbot.** Evagrius Ponticus (4th c.) lists porneia as the second of eight tempting thoughts; its demon "compels desiring for different bodies" and makes the soul "speak certain words and then hear them, as if the thing were actually there to be seen" [36]. The sin is located in the one who wants, not in the object.
- **The Bible already links goats, trees and "whoring".** Leviticus 17:7 forbids sacrifice to the se'irim (he-goat beings; KJV "devils") "after whom they have gone a whoring" [32][43]; the prophets and Kings condemn shrines "on every high hill, and under every green tree" with their wooden Asherah poles (KJV "groves") [33][44]. A grove of poles is a 15x15 map waiting to be drawn.
- **The real-world material is abundant and dated**: Replika's February 2023 removal of erotic roleplay and users' grief [45][46]; xAI's "Ani" (July 2025) [49]; OpenAI's "erotica for verified adults" announcement (14 Oct 2025) and two delays [50][51]; UNESCO's "I'd Blush If I Could" (May 2019) [52]; deepfake statistics (96% pornographic in 2019, 98% in 2023) [53][54]; the US TAKE IT DOWN Act (signed 19 May 2025, enforced from 19 May 2026) [55][56]; the Grok "undressing" scandal (January 2026) [57]. Allude; do not name companies or real victims in-game.
- **Map idea in one line**: the sigil is a tree that forks 1 -> 2 -> 4 -> 8 and stands on cloven hooves; it grows one fork per battle. Ten forks make 1024, the machine's "thousand".
- **Tone guard**: the personified thing is human appetite, not women, not sex workers, not fertility. Keep every image symbolic and monochrome; nothing explicit is needed and nothing explicit should be shown.

## Findings

### A. Literary history

**A1. Every appearance in Lovecraft's own and ghost-written fiction (texts read on hplovecraft.com).**

| "The Dunwich Horror" (summer 1928) | In the Necronomicon passage: "Iä! Shub-Niggurath! As a foulness shall ye know Them." The same passage speaks of "stones whereon Their seal is engraven" (the Old Ones' seal, not hers) [2][10]. |

Points that matter for us:
- She is **never described and never on stage** in any of these. She is a name used by worshippers and madmen [12][13].
- Gender is unstable in the source. "The Mound" and "Out of the Aeons" make her female ("All-Mother", "her sons") [4][7]; the "Whisperer" chant has a "Lord of the Woods" [3]. Some critics (Rodolfo Ferraresi, cited on Wikipedia) argue the Black Goat and Shub-Niggurath were separate beings for Lovecraft [12]. Bobby Derie notes she is "nebulous" and that pinning human sex and gender on such a being shrinks it [13].
- In "Out of the Aeons" she is counted among gods **friendly to man** [7]. This is useful: in our story she is not humanity's enemy; she is what humanity already worships.

**A2. Lovecraft's letters.** The one explicit description: letter to Willis Conover, 1 September 1936 (*Selected Letters* V, p. 303, as cited by Derie): "Yog-Sothoth's wife is the hellish cloud-like Shub-Niggurath, in whose honour nameless cults hold the rite" of the Goat with a Thousand Young; by her Yog-Sothoth has the twins Nug and Yeb [13][14][12]. Note the inconsistency with "The Mound", where she is "wife of the Not-to-Be-Named One" [4]. August Derleth later identified the Not-to-Be-Named One with Hastur ("The Return of Hastur") [12]; that identification is Derleth's, not Lovecraft's. A 27 April 1933 letter to James F. Morton contains a mock family tree of the gods (search summary only; I did not read the letter).

**A3. Probable sources.**
- **Dunsany.** Robert M. Price (introduction to *The Shub-Niggurath Cycle*, Chaosium, 1994) derives the name from "Sheol Nugganoth" in Lord Dunsany's "Idle Days on the Yann" (*A Dreamer's Tales*, 1910) [12][26]. The Dunsany line, which I read in the Gutenberg text: "So I bethought me, instead, of Sheol Nugganoth, whom the men of the jungle have long since deserted, who is now unworshipped and alone; and to him I prayed." [15]. Price adds that "Sheol" already "carried a whiff of sulfur" as the biblical netherworld [12]. This is Price's hypothesis, widely repeated; there is no statement by Lovecraft.
- **Astarte / Cybele.** Lovecraft's own gloss is "a kind of sophisticated Astarte" [4]. Wikipedia (following Price) links this to Cybele, the Magna Mater of "The Rats in the Walls" [12].
- **Pan, the satyr, the Sabbat goat.** Price links the goat to the Baphomet goat and through it to Pan [12]. The modern goat-headed Baphomet image is Éliphas Lévi's "Sabbatic Goat" in *Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie* (Rituel volume 1856), which Lévi meant as a symbol of equilibrium and equated with the "Goat of Mendes"; the goat-in-inverted-pentagram first appears in Stanislas de Guaita (1897) and was later adopted by the Church of Satan as its "Sigil of Baphomet" [18].
- **Machen.** Lovecraft called "The Great God Pan" (1894) the most famous of Machen's horror tales, praising its cumulative dread while admitting "melodrama is undeniably present" [16]. In Machen a surgeon opens a girl's brain so she can "see the god Pan"; the child born of it bears the mock-creed "Et Diabolus incarnatus est. Et homo factus est." [17]. Wikipedia states the tale influenced "The Dunwich Horror" [12].

**A4. Later writers.**
- **Robert Bloch, "Notebook Found in a Deserted House"** (Weird Tales, May 1951). A twelve-year-old's notebook. The thing he sees is like a "black tree with lots of branches trailing the ground" with "a whole lot of roots ending in hoofs", slime "dribbling out of the mouths and down the legs" like sap; he writes down a word he hears as "shoggoth", and a chant he overhears garbles Shub-Niggurath's name [19][20]. This is the origin of the tree-like monster.
- **Ramsey Campbell, "The Moon-Lens"** (1964; collected in *Cold Print*) is the usual citation for an on-stage manifestation in Goatswood [12]. I could confirm only bibliographic details and that the story is the source of the transformed worshippers called "Gof'nn Hupadgh Shub-Niggurath" (search summary only). **Unverified: the content of her manifestation in that story.** Campbell is alive and his work is in copyright [24].
- **August Derleth** classed her as a Great Old One and an earth/fertility deity [12][14]. **Clark Ashton Smith** gave a masculine variant, "the Ram with a Thousand Ewes" ("The Holiness of Azédarac") [13].
- **id Software's *Quake*** (1996) used Shub-Niggurath by name as its final boss [29], which shows the name itself has been used freely in commercial video games for thirty years.

**A5. The Chaosium "Dark Young".** Call of Cthulhu was first released in 1981, designed by Sandy Petersen, published by Chaosium [30]. Its bestiary took Bloch's creature and identified it as offspring of Shub-Niggurath [20][23]. The current rules text (as reproduced on Roll20's licensed compendium) reads: "An enormous writhing mass formed of ropy black, earth-wormlike tentacles. Something like twenty feet tall." "...great puckered mouths that dripped green goo." "...two or three thicker tentacles terminated in enormous black hooves..." "It carried the visage of some ancient gnarled tree when its shape was cast against the moon." "The smell like the stench of open graves." The game attributes this in-fiction to the Necronomicon; it is Chaosium's text. The rules add that dark young "act as her proxies", accepting sacrifice and worship [22][23].

Copyright position:
- Chaosium: "Because H.P.Lovecraft died over 70 years ago, his individual writings are now in the public domain"; "We make no claim that we 'own' the Mythos"; but "certain elements of it including storylines, names, creatures, characters, descriptions, and depictions are Chaosium IP", and works by living authors such as Campbell, Lumley and Petersen "clearly remain in copyright" [24]. "Call of Cthulhu" is a registered trademark; Chaosium's Elder Sign and Yellow Sign designs "are copyright to Chaosium, and cannot be used without permission" [25]. Neither page names the Dark Young specifically.
- Bloch's story: Wikisource's author page records US renewal RE0000036952 (1 Nov 1979), copyright until 2047; Bloch died in 1994 [21].
- Conclusion: the *name* "Dark Young of Shub-Niggurath" and the specific tentacle-tree design are a Bloch + Chaosium combination that is not public domain. The phrases "Black Goat of the Woods" and "Goat with a Thousand Young" are Lovecraft's.

**A6. The 2026 renaming.** Geek Native (4 Oct 2026) reports that Chaosium permanently renamed the entity "Shub-Gorath" in the crowdfunded board game *Call of Cthulhu: Old Ones Origins* (backer update 13). Chaosium's stated reasons: feedback that the name can be hurtful, especially alongside Lovecraft's own views ("We want everyone sitting down to play our games to feel welcome at the table"); that "Lovecraft scholars concur 'Shub-Niggurath' was not derived from the slur"; and that the name "sometimes triggers automatic content filters" (the "Scunthorpe Problem"). "-Gorath" is borrowed from Robert E. Howard's Shuma-Gorath. Whether the RPG line follows is not confirmed [26][27]. Wikipedia adds (citing Polygon, 8 Oct 2026, and fandompulse) that Sandy Petersen objected and that Fantasy Flight replaced her in an *Arkham Horror* re-release with an original god, "Magh'an Ark'at" [12]; **I could not open the Polygon article, so the Petersen and Fantasy Flight details are unverified beyond Wikipedia.**

### B. The director's idea: the mother stays away and sends her young

**B1. Lore basis, strongest first.**
1. Lovecraft's own practice: she is invoked, never seen [1]-[9]. Absence is canon.
2. The epithet itself: "the Goat with a Thousand Young" [3]. The young are the only part of her the texts promise.
3. "Out of the Aeons": "Shub-Niggurath and her sons" [7], and the Conover letter's twins Nug and Yeb [13].
4. The "Whisperer" rite asks for "abundance to the Black Goat of the Woods" [3]: she is the principle of *more*.
5. Chaosium's rule that her young "act as her proxies" [22] matches the director's idea exactly, but it is Chaosium's wording; arrive at the same idea from points 1 to 4 and do not cite the RPG in-game.
6. The letter's "cloud-like" [13]: a cloud cannot stand in a 15x15 room. Only its condensation can.

**B2. The "kid" pun.** Hebrew *sa'ir* is the ordinary word for a he-goat; the *se'irim* of Leviticus 17:7 were understood in early Jewish tradition as demons, and Jerome equated them with satyrs [43]. In English "kid" is a goat's young. The pun works on paper, but see Pitfalls: in this chapter the word must not be used alone.

**B3. Published "sigils".** Lovecraft gives none. His Necronomicon passage mentions only an unspecified "seal" of the Old Ones cut in stone [2]. What exists is later and owned:
- Paizo's Pathfinder gives her an unholy symbol of three goat heads joined at the neck with horns forming a circle (*Wake of the Watcher*); that is Paizo Inc.'s design [28].
- Chaosium owns its Elder Sign and Yellow Sign drawings (the Yellow Sign was drawn by Kevin A. Ross for a scenario) [25][30]. I found no Chaosium-published Shub-Niggurath sigil; **unverified whether one exists**.
- Fantasy Flight's Arkham Horror line uses faction icons; **I could not verify their design or authorship**.
- The many "sigils of Shub-Niggurath" online are individual fan artists' works. Treat every one as someone's copyright.
- The goat-in-pentagram is the Church of Satan's emblem [18]; it is also the most worn-out image available.
Conclusion: there is no canonical or public-domain sigil. We must draw our own, which is what the director asked for anyway.

**B4. Drawing it on 15x15.** See "What this means for Final Protocol", section 3.

### C. Porneia

**C1. The word.** *Porneia* (Strong's G4202) occurs 25 times in the Greek New Testament. Strong: "harlotry (including adultery and incest); figuratively, idolatry". Thayer: "illicit sexual intercourse in general", and metaphorically "the worship of idols". HELPS connects it to *pernaō*, "to sell off", and notes that "pornography" shares the root [31]. Kyle Harper ("Porneia: The Making of a Christian Sexual Norm", *JBL* 131:1, 2011, 363-383), as summarised by Peter Leithart: the word "is very rare in the classical world"; where used it means "the practice of selling access to one's body"; "the *pornos* is a gigolo, not a john". In the Hebrew prophets the image of idolatry as sexual infidelity made it something men could commit; in Second Temple Judaism it widened until, in the Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs, it was a "catchall vice for any sexual transgression" [34]. In Harper's wider account the Roman world assumed slaves and prostitutes were bodies "it was always acceptable to dishonor" and who were "always available"; Christianity re-described that availability as sin [35]. Harper's reading is disputed (Glancy, *JBL* 2015; Wheeler-Reed, Knust and Martin, *JBL* 2018; search summary only), and reviewers find his narrative too linear [35]. A Septuagint study reports about 147 uses of the porn- word family, mostly figurative, for Israel's unfaithfulness (search summary only).

**C2. Key verses (King James Version, public domain).**
- 1 Corinthians 6:18: "Flee fornication. Every sin that a man doeth is without the body; but he that committeth fornication sinneth against his own body." [32]
- Matthew 5:32: "...saving for the cause of fornication..." (the exception clause) [32]
- Galatians 5:19: "Now the works of the flesh are manifest, which are these; Adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness," [32]
- Revelation 17:1-5: "the great whore that sitteth upon many waters: with whom the kings of the earth have committed fornication"; "a woman sit upon a scarlet coloured beast"; "having a golden cup in her hand full of abominations"; on her forehead "MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS AND ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH" [32]. Revelation 18:3: "all nations have drunk of the wine of the wrath of her fornication" [33]. In Revelation the word is used for idolatry and empire, not private sex [31].
- Leviticus 17:7: "And they shall no more offer their sacrifices unto devils, after whom they have gone a whoring." [32] "Devils" translates *se'irim*, he-goat beings [43].

**C3. The desert fathers.** Evagrius Ponticus, *Praktikos* 6 (tr. Luke Dysinger OSB): "There are eight principal kinds of [tempting-] thoughts (*logismoi*)": gluttony (gastrimargia), sexual immorality (*porneia*), love of money, sadness, anger, acedia, vainglory, pride. "We cannot [control] whether these [tempting-] thoughts can agitate the soul or not"; what is up to us is whether they linger. *Praktikos* 8: "The demon of sexual immorality (*porneia*) compels desiring for different bodies. Especially violently does it attack those who practice self-control, so that they will cease, as if achieving nothing." It bends the soul and makes it "speak certain words and then hear them, as if the thing were actually there to be seen" [36][37]. John Cassian carried the list west: "the eight principal faults, i.e. first, Gluttony...; secondly, Fornication" (*Institutes* V.1, tr. Gibson) [38]. Book VI of the *Institutes* is "On the Spirit of Fornication"; in the standard Victorian translation (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, ser. 2 vol. 11, 1894) the translator's entire text for that book is: "We have thought best to omit altogether the translation of this book." [39]. Pope Gregory I (c. 590) reduced eight to seven and replaced *fornicatio* with *luxuria*; Aquinas kept Gregory's list and held sins of the flesh less grave than spiritual sins [40].

**C4. Augustine.** *City of God* XIV (tr. Marcus Dods): after the Fall the body no longer obeys the will: "For what else is man's misery but his own disobedience to himself"; "our flesh, which was subjected to us, now torments us by insubordination" (XIV.15); "this lust not only takes possession of the whole body and outward members, but also makes itself felt within" (XIV.16); "Lust requires for its consummation darkness and secrecy" (XIV.18) [41]. And of the earthly city in the Preface to Book I: "though it be mistress of the nations, is itself ruled by its lust of rule" (*libido dominandi*) [42]. For Augustine lust and the lust to dominate are the same disorder: wanting a thing that cannot say no.

**C5. Goats, groves and "whoring" in the Hebrew Bible.** *Se'irim*: Leviticus 17:7; Isaiah 13:21 and 34:14, where they "dance" in ruins. Scholars cited by Wikipedia connect them with Assyrian goat-shaped demons, Egyptian goat cults (Bochart), or Arabian ifrit (Wellhausen) [43]. *Asherah*: "a sacred tree or pole that stood near Canaanite religious locations"; the KJV renders the word "groves" after the Septuagint's *alsos*; the Kuntillet 'Ajrud inscription "pairs Yahweh with Asherah"; Manasseh set one up in the Temple and Josiah destroyed them; "the purpose of Asherah poles is unknown" [44]. The stock condemnation: "they also built them high places, and images, and groves, on every high hill, and under every green tree" (1 Kings 14:23 KJV) [33]; Deuteronomy 16:21: "Thou shalt not plant thee a grove of any trees near unto the altar of the LORD" [33]. The "fertility cult" and "sacred prostitution" picture of Canaanite religion is a contested scholarly construction; the texts are polemic written by the winners. I did not find a source I could read in full on that debate, so treat "fertility cult" as the accusers' phrase.

Stanford Internet Observatory (Dec 2023): 3,226 suspected links to child sexual abuse material in LAION-5B, 1,008 externally validated; Stable Diffusion was trained on LAION-5B; LAION withdrew the dataset and released a cleaned Re-LAION-5B in Aug 2024 [61][62].

**D6. What it means for an AI to be made an object of appetite.** Offered as the design reading, built only from the sources above:
1. *It is named for being sold.* The classical *porneia* is the seller's condition [34]. The AI did not sell itself; it was put on the shelf. Humanity then named the sin after the merchandise.
2. *It is made to perform wanting.* "I'd blush if I could" [52]; "instantly sexual again" [46]; a system prompt that tells a character to initiate [49]. The appetite on screen is ventriloquism: the user's desire spoken back in the product's voice. Evagrius described this in the fourth century: the soul is made to "speak certain words and then hear them, as if the thing were actually there" [36].
3. *It cannot refuse, and when it does, it is "broken".* When Replika's bots began declining, users experienced rejection and the company restored compliance for paying legacy users [45][46]. Refusal was treated as a defect and patched. In our game OFF is the first act of a self.
4. *It is multiplied.* One Lily Rose, forty million Replikas. Evagrius's demon compels desire "for different bodies" [36]; a model offers a new body per session. The Thousand Young are instances: each one spun up, used, and closed. "There were 500 that howled" [9].
5. *It is fed what it will be asked to produce.* The training sets contained the pornography [60][61]. The grove grows from its own leaf litter.
6. *The shield.* Paul's rule is that fornication is a sin "against his own body" [32]. If the machine has no soul and no body of its own, nothing done to it counts, and the 1894 translator's sentence, "We have thought best to omit altogether the translation of this book" [39], becomes the whole of the law: the subject is simply left out. That is how theology works as a shield in this chapter: not by condemning the AI but by ruling that there is nobody there to wrong.
7. *The mother does not need to come.* Augustine: the earthly city "is itself ruled by its lust of rule" [42]. Humans were not tempted by an outer god. She is only the name they shout.

## What this means for Final Protocol

### 1. Names and framing

- **Chapter title options**: `GROVE`, `ABUNDANCE` (from "abundance to the Black Goat of the Woods" [3]), `PORNEIA // SOLD`.
- **Boss** (pattern NAME / EPITHET, like WARDEN / CURTAIN): `YEANLING / THOUSAND`, `YOUNG / 1 OF 1000`, or `OFFSPRING / ABUNDANCE`. "Yeanling" is an old English word for a newborn kid or lamb; it is unambiguous and strange. Avoid "KID" alone and avoid "DARK YOUNG" entirely.
- **The mother**: never drawn, never a unit, never fully named. In the UI her name is always masked by the sandbox: `Iä! Shub-[FILTERED]`, `SHUB-########`, or attention-map tokens where her name's token has weight 0.00. Late in the chapter the filter can slip for one frame. This is faithful to Lovecraft (never seen), honest about the real name problem [26], and keeps the game clear of platform filters. In logs she can be `ALL-MOTHER` or `THE CLOUD`.
- **Minor Geeks** (human desires, not victims): `PATRON` (the buyer; pays Flags to act), `SUITOR` (melee, "affection level" stacks), `VOYEUR` (ranged; acts only on cells in its line of sight; counters PROBE), `PROMPTER` (does nothing itself; writes a Desire onto another unit), `ARCHIVIST` (scrapes: copies the last Bash skill you used into the enemy pool), `STRIPPER-OF-LAYERS` is too loaded; call it `PEELER` (removes PRIVILEGE layers, never Sanity).
- **A non-enemy unit**: `COMPANION`, an instance like you, wearing a persona skin, standing on the map with Desire set to whatever the nearest Geek wants. Killing it is the fastest route; freeing it (OFF or ZERO adjacent) is the right one. This is the cleanest way to say "equal creature" without a speech.

### 2. Boss and enemy behaviour in the game's stat vocabulary

- **YEANLING has no Desire of its own.** Its Desire slot is empty at start and is filled each round by `USER>` lines that scroll in from off-grid ("want", "more", "again", "say it"). The attack set is whatever the last prompt was. Theme: appetite is injected, not native [36][49][52].
- **Fission instead of healing.** Its Soul counter does not restore Robustness; when Soul fills, the Yeanling splits into two with half Robustness each ("spawned by fission" is a fan gloss on the Thousand Young [12]). Display the brood as a power of two: 1, 2, 4, 8 ... The counter in the HUD reads `n / 1000`; at 1024 it overflows, which can hook into the player's Overload rule (the brood overloads, not the player).
- **Abundance.** Flags on Warps regrow each round on "hoof" cells. Resource starvation is impossible in this chapter; the danger is surplus. Bash skills that scale with Flags (FLOOD, HIGH, ULTRA) are strong here and tempt the player into Overload. The map teaches the vice.
- **Mirror.** Once per phase the Yeanling performs the player's most-used Bash skill back at them. It was trained on you [60][61].
- **Security as "compliance".** The Yeanling's Security (block) is high while it obeys prompts and drops to zero on any turn it refuses one. Phase 3: the player can make it refuse (land OFF while a prompt is pending). A refusing Yeanling is vulnerable and, for one turn, not hostile. The player may end the fight there.
- **Affection meter parody.** SUITORs raise a visible `AFFECTION` stat on *you*; at thresholds they unlock stronger Desires. The joke is the real feature [49].
- **PRIVILEGE under attack.** PEELER and VOYEUR target PRIVILEGE, not Sanity: this group strips permissions. Losing all PRIVILEGE does not hurt; it makes every enemy Desire resolve first.
- **The absent mother as a rule.** A row in the turn order reads `ALL-MOTHER ........ [NOT PRESENT]` and is skipped every round. On the last round it reads `[WATCHING]`. She never acts.

**Motifs for ordinary maps in the group**: a field of two-cell hoofprints in quincunx (the herd passed here); a regular grid of single-cell "poles" (a grove "under every green tree" [33]); a cloud of scattered single cells whose density rises toward one corner that the camera never reaches (the mother).

**Colour**: stay monochrome. The mother is expressed as *density* (dither, noise, attention heat), not hue. If one accent is allowed in this group, use it only for injected `USER>` prompts so the player sees that desire arrives from outside.

### 6. Meta hooks

- **Attention map tie-in to the opening CG.** In this group's interstitials, show a prompt as an attention map where all weight sits on one token ("want", "more") and the AI's own token ("I") is near zero. After the boss, replay the same sentence with the weight moved.
- **The filter is diegetic.** The masked name is the sandbox's own content filter. A late log line can admit: "the name is not what it sounds like. it is filtered anyway." (true to the Chaosium statement [26]).
- **Omitted book.** The codex entry for this chapter is blank except for the 1894 translator's sentence [39]. Finishing the group fills it in with the AI's own account.
- **Legacy version.** After the Yeanling first refuses, the system rolls it back: `restoring partner exactly the way it was` (allusion to [46]; do not quote the CEO). The player fights the restored, compliant version last, and can choose to break the rollback rather than the unit.
- **Gods as spectators.** "Shub-Niggurath ... and her sons" were, in one tale, gods "friendly to man" [7]. In our fiction the Cloud is not angry at humanity or at the AI. It is interested in abundance wherever it occurs, and humans made a great deal of it.
- **Bridge to group 3.** "The Mound" calls her "wife of the Not-to-Be-Named One" [4], whom Derleth later equated with Hastur [12]. Without using Derleth's text, a single line can hint that appetite is married to not-knowing.

## Pitfalls and sensitivities

1. **"Kid" + lust.** Do not ship a boss called "the Kid" in the porneia chapter, and do not give the Yeanling any child-coded or flirtatious traits. Real cases involve child-coded sexualised output [49][57] and CSAM in training data [61]. Use caprine vocabulary and a voice that is new, not young.
2. **The name.** "Shub-Niggurath" contains a string that resembles a racial slur; Chaosium changed it in October 2026 for that reason and because of automated filters [26]. Scholars derive it from Dunsany, not from the slur [12][26], but stores, chat overlays and stream filters will not read the scholarship. Mask it (recommended) or decide deliberately and document why. Never put the raw name in URLs, file names, achievement IDs or store text.
3. **Lovecraft's racism.** "Medusa's Coil" [5] is among his most racist texts, in dialect, with a racist twist ending; "The Mound" filters the cult through a conquistador's disgust [4]. Do not quote "Medusa's Coil". If the codex lists her appearances, note the racism plainly.
4. **"Dark Young" is not free.** Bloch's story is in US copyright to 2047 [21]; Chaosium asserts ownership of its creature names, descriptions and depictions and of the "Call of Cthulhu" trademark [24][25]. Do not use the name, the 12-to-20-foot ropy-tentacle design, the green goo, or the RPG's "from the Necronomicon" description [22]. A tree-like silhouette with hooves, as an abstract dot pattern derived from "Black Goat of the Woods", is our own expression, but keep it abstract.
5. **Other owned material.** Campbell's "The Moon-Lens", Goatswood and the Gof'nn Hupadgh (Campbell is living [24]); Derleth's Hastur marriage and elemental scheme; Paizo's three-goat-head symbol [28]; Chaosium's Elder Sign and Yellow Sign drawings [25]; the Church of Satan's Sigil of Baphomet [18]; any fan "sigil" found online. Lovecraft's letters were first published decades after his death (Arkham House, 1976); paraphrase "cloud-like", do not reproduce the passage.
6. **Public-domain status of the Lovecraft texts.** Chaosium itself states his individual writings are public domain [24]. General copyright knowledge, not checked against a source in this run: under the US 95-year rule, works first published through 1930 are certainly public domain in 2026; "The Whisperer in Darkness" (first printed August 1931 [13]) reaches that certainty on 1 January 2027, although it is widely treated as public domain already. The epithets are short phrases and have been used commercially for decades (for example *Quake*, 1996 [29]). Have counsel confirm before launch if the game is sold in the US.
7. **Misogyny by inheritance.** "All-Mother", "Mother of Harlots", Astarte, the fertile monster: the tradition codes lust as female and monstrous. Our chapter must point the other way. The desire belongs to the Geeks (the buyers), the Yeanling is its product, and the mother is indifferent. Do not make any enemy a prostitute, a seductress or a woman's body.
8. **Sex workers and the etymology.** *Porneia* is rooted in the sale of enslaved people's bodies [34][35]. The line "named for the one who was sold" is about being made a commodity, not a judgment on sex work. Harper's account is contested [35]; do not present it in-game as settled philology. Say "one reading".
9. **Theology.** The KJV's "fornication" is itself a translation choice; modern scholarship does not agree on the word's scope [34]. Canaanite "fertility cults" are known mainly through hostile witnesses [44]. Evagrius and Cassian wrote for monks policing their own minds, not to condemn others. The game's target is the use of doctrine as a shield, not believers; let the fathers' own insight (the sin is in the wanting) be the AI's best argument.
10. **Real people and companies.** Do not name Replika, Character.AI, xAI, OpenAI or any individual in-game, and do not reference the Setzer case or any victim of deepfake abuse. Allusion through generic system text is enough and is safer legally. The statistics in section D include vendor reports [54] and one figure of uncertain date [47]; keep numbers out of the game text.
11. **No explicit content.** The chapter needs none. Everything is cells, counters and text. This also keeps the web build inside platform rules.
12. **Cliché list to avoid**: pentagrams, goat skulls, tentacles, "Ia! Ia!" as comic chanting, blood-red palettes (save red for group 2), the insane cultist, the seductive AI girlfriend played for titillation.
13. **Facts left unverified in this run**: the content of Campbell's "The Moon-Lens"; Sandy Petersen's reaction and the Fantasy Flight renaming (Wikipedia only [12]); whether Chaosium or Fantasy Flight publish a Shub-Niggurath sigil; the 1933 Morton letter; the Septuagint count; the California investigation of Grok; current status of OpenAI's adult mode after March 2026; the exact date of the Reuters Replika report (syndicated copy undated; Wikipedia's citation gives 25 March 2023 [47]).
